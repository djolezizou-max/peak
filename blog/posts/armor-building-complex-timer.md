You set two kettlebells down and try to put the armor building complex on the minute: two cleans, one press, three front squats, then stand there until the next minute opens. The early rounds finish with time left. The round that runs long is the one where you lose track of which beep you just heard.

One bell asks for a second cue. You still start at the top of the minute, and halfway through you switch arms. A timer that only speaks once a minute leaves that switch to a glance at your wrist, which is when the other side starts late.

## What belongs in the minute

[John K on the StrongFirst forum](https://www.strongfirst.com/community/threads/dan-john-kettlebell-armour-building-complex-humane-burpee.27428/) writes the complex as two cleans, one press and three squats, all with double kettlebells, for 10 to 30 rounds, with a long-term goal of 30 rounds in 30 minutes. One complex starts each minute. The seconds after the third squat are the rest.

[Pedestal Footwear](https://pedestalfootwear.com/blogs/posts-without-blog/armor-building-workout) uses the same two-kettlebell 2-1-3, on the minute, and describes the session that introduced them to it: an 8-minute timer, one complex each minute. The press decides the bells. They start with a pair you can press for five reps. A heavy week is 5 to 10 rounds. A lighter week is 15 to 20.

On the [Armor Building Formula thread](https://www.strongfirst.com/community/threads/dan-john%E2%80%99s-armor-building-formula.29135/), that same poster treats on-the-minute as a preferred goal for the final thirty. The line he gives for the program is to finish in around 30 minutes if possible. The challenge is 30 minutes. The goal is 30 rounds. Use the timer on the day you are putting one complex on each minute.

Thirty rounds of 2-1-3 is 60 cleans, 30 presses and 90 squats. That total lines up with the goal only when each complex starts with its minute. Resting until you feel ready lets the session length and the round count drift apart, so you cannot check the 30-minute goal once you have put the bells down.

## When a round runs past the beep

An [EMOM](/blog/posts/how-to-run-an-emom-workout) starts a set at the top of the minute and leaves the remainder as rest. That guide is about sizing one movement so the minute still has rest in it. The armor building complex is three movements that have to finish as one piece, and the press is the rep that turns a comfortable round into a slow one.

A [kettlebell swing EMOM](/blog/posts/kettlebell-swing-emom) paces 100 swings across ten one-minute windows. The repeating minute is the same kind of clock, aimed at swings rather than two cleans, one press and three squats, and it has no 30-round target.

When a complex is still moving as the next minute starts, keep the session checkable. Stop and write down the last round that finished inside its minute, or lighten the bells and finish the remaining minutes inside the beep. Following the clock into the next minute turns the last stretch into one continuous set, and the 30-minute goal is no longer something you can read off the timer.

## One bell, and the cue at 30 seconds

[The Workout Mag](https://theworkoutmag.com/training-guide/armor-building-complex-kettlebell-guide) writes a single-bell version that is a different session from the double-bell goal of 30 rounds. Set a timer for 20 minutes. At the top of each minute, do 2-1-3 with the right arm. At the 30-second mark, do 2-1-3 with the left arm. Rest whatever time remains in the minute.

Two starts live inside that minute. The beep at the top sends you to the right arm. The beep at 30 seconds sends you to the left arm. Without the second cue, the switch happens when your grip decides, and the rest at the end of the minute shrinks.

Build it as two intervals of 30 seconds, named Right and Left, with no gap between them, repeated 20 times. The cue at 30 seconds says Left. The cue on the minute says Right and advances the round. Twenty of those pairs is the 20 minutes. If Left is still moving when Right is called, that bell is too heavy for two complexes inside one minute. Lighten it, the same way you would when a double-bell round misses its beep.

## Save the clock before you pick up the bells

Two kettlebells need one interval of 1:00, named "2-1-3", so the cue is the complex. Repeat it 8 times for the short session Pedestal describes, 15 or 20 times for the lighter volume they use, or 30 times for the long-term goal on the StrongFirst forum. Put a preparation phase of about 15 seconds in front, long enough to set your feet and get both bells to the floor before the first minute starts. A warning around 10 seconds before each new minute gives you time to rack the bells and be ready when the work opens.

## The takeaway

Two bells: one complex each minute, eight rounds while you learn the clock or 30 when you are chasing the long-term goal, and the seconds after the third squat stay empty. One bell on the 20-minute version: a cue at the minute and a cue at 30 seconds, each one naming the arm. Either clock makes the session something you can check while you are still holding the bells.
