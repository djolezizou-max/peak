You set a beep and ran the shuttle. Every leg came out the same length. The pace never rose, you lost track of the level, and the thing you finished was a shuttle repeat on a fixed gap.

A beep test is a 20m shuttle whose gap shrinks. Beep Test Online and beeptest.net describe a start near 8.5 km/h, about 8.5 seconds for that first shuttle on the Léger protocol, and a shorter allowance at every level after it. There is no walk between shuttles. The score is the last level and shuttle. One late shuttle is a warning. Two late shuttles in a row end the test.

## What the beep is measuring

Each beep is the moment one 20m leg is due. You run to the line, turn, and the next leg is already counting. At the Léger start that leg is about 8.5 seconds, the allowance that goes with a pace near 8.5 km/h. The next level gives you less time for the same 20 metres, and the level after that gives you less again. Nothing in the test inserts a recovery jog.

The result is not a total of minutes. It is the last level you reached and the shuttle inside that level. The first time you arrive after the beep, that shuttle is the warning, and the test is still going. The next late shuttle, straight after that one, ends it. You stop there and write down the last level and shuttle.

## A fixed gap is a different session

A normal interval timer holds every shuttle at the same length. Dedicated beep-test timers exist for that reason. thetimerlab.com is one of them, and Beep Test Online and beeptest.net describe the same shrinking shuttle, because a fixed gap is not this test. Leave every shuttle at the opening length, about 8.5 seconds, and you never leave the first pace. The test is the shortening.

Equal hard and easy repeats are a different run. That session is an [interval running workout](/blog/posts/interval-running-workout-timer): both sides are filled in before you start, and they stay filled in. The 30-15 intermittent fitness test is different again. It keeps a fixed 30 seconds of running and 15 seconds of walking, and it raises the speed inside that pair. The beep test has no walk, and the number that changes is the time you are given for each 20m shuttle.

## Give every level its own shorter shuttle

In Peak Interval a level is one named interval, repeated once for each shuttle in that level, with no rest between the shuttles and no rest before the next level. The next level is the next interval, and its duration is shorter than the one you just finished. Name them Level 1, Level 2, and so on. The spoken name is how you hear that the beeps just got closer. A beep that sounds the same at every boundary leaves you counting levels in your head, which is the count the test takes away from you.

Take the later shuttle times, and how many shuttles sit in each level, from the protocol you are being scored on. Beep Test Online and beeptest.net describe that protocol. The only time this page will put on the clock is the Léger opening those sources give: about 8.5 seconds. Every level after that is a shorter interval than the one before it.

Do not set one work length and a round count for the whole test. That is the timer that made every shuttle the same length. A preparation phase of about 15 seconds at the front is enough to put your foot on the line before the first shuttle starts. Save the workout. The next attempt should be the same clock, or the level you write down is not comparable to the last one.

## You still judge the late shuttle

The beep says the line was due. It does not know whether you got there. You do. One late arrival is the warning, and the following shuttle is still part of the test. Two late arrivals in a row are the end. Stop the workout on that second miss and record the last level and shuttle. If the timer keeps calling the next level, that level is no longer your score.

Say the level name out loud at the change, and let the shuttles inside the level share one beep. Two words is as much as you will catch once the gap is tight. On an Apple Watch, start the saved workout from the wrist. The tap at each shuttle still lands when the gym is loud, and the watch face does not have to be readable for you to know a beep was due. The level name is the part that tells you the gap changed.

Stand on the line and play the first level before you use the clock for a score. Lock the phone. You should hear the level, hear nothing that inserts a walk, and hear the next level start on a shorter shuttle.

## The takeaway

Mark 20 metres. Open at about 8.5 seconds a shuttle. Shorten that time at every new level, with no walk between shuttles, and have the timer say the level. Stop on the second late shuttle in a row, and write down the last level and shuttle.
