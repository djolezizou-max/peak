You have done EMOMs, and you know the deal: a set starts at the top of every minute and you rest whatever is left. Then a workout says "E2MOM 20" and you realise the rules are the same but the problem is not. Two minutes is a lot of clock. Put too little in it and you are standing around for 80 seconds. Put too much in it and you are still going when the next window opens.

The other snag is the timer. Most EMOM timers are built around a one-minute window, so people end up running E2MOM off the gym wall clock, counting even minutes in their head. This guide covers what actually belongs in a two-minute window, how much of it, and how to set the clock so you never have to count.

## Size the work to 60-80 seconds

In a normal EMOM the work sits around 30-40 seconds, leaving 20-30 seconds of rest. E2MOM keeps roughly the same ratio, so the target is **60-80 seconds of work and 40-60 seconds of rest** in each window. That is the range where the last set still looks like the first one.

The easy way to size it: do one set at the start of the session and time it. If it takes under 50 seconds, add reps or load. If it takes over 90, cut it back. Over 90 seconds leaves you under 30 seconds to recover, and by window six you will be starting late.

A stretch of the window is not a reason to double the effort. "E2MOM" does not mean "twice the reps of your EMOM". Two minutes buys you either heavier work, more movements, or longer work, and you pick one.

## What fits in the window

**Heavy strength sets.** This is the classic use. Three to five front squats, deadlifts or push presses at around 75-85% of your max take 20-30 seconds to lift, but you need the extra recovery to do it again at that weight. Here the work is short on purpose and the rest is long. A typical example is E2MOM 20 with 3 front squats: ten heavy sets with nobody counting rest.

**Two movements in one window.** A one-minute window cannot fit 10 kettlebell swings and 10 burpees without turning into a sprint. Two minutes can. Pair a strength movement with a bodyweight one, like 8 dumbbell thrusters and 8 burpees, or 12 swings and 10 push-ups, and aim for the pair to finish around 70 seconds.

**A buy-in run or row.** Start each window with a 200 m run or a 250 m row, then a short set: 200 m run plus 10 wall balls, for example. The run is typically 45-55 seconds, which leaves 20-30 seconds for the set and a short break. This is the version that falls apart fastest if the run is too long, so time your first one.

## How to set the clock

Underneath, an E2MOM is one interval of 2:00, repeated. Divide the total time by two and you have the number of rounds: E2MOM 20 is ten rounds, E2MOM 16 is eight. You do not need a work phase and a rest phase, because the rest is whatever the window leaves.

What you do want is a cue before the boundary. In a two-minute window, the long rest is where people drift off: you sit down, check your phone, and look up at 1:58. A spoken or haptic warning 10 seconds before the next window gives you time to get chalk, find your grip and be ready at 0:00.

If you are pairing movements, name the interval after both ("thrusters + burpees") so the wrist tells you the job when you are too tired to remember it. For an E2MOM that alternates two different windows, say heavy squats in odd windows and a row in even ones, make two 2:00 intervals, name each one, and set five rounds.

## When E3MOM makes more sense

If your set is consistently pushing past 90 seconds and you do not want to cut it, the window is too short. Move to E3MOM: same rules, three-minute windows, and work sized to about 90-120 seconds. That is the right home for longer chippers, a 400 m run plus a set, or heavy complexes of three movements.

The rule of thumb works both ways. If your work fits easily under 40 seconds, an E2MOM is probably wasting time and a normal EMOM is the better format.

## The short version

An E2MOM is a set every two minutes. Fill the window with 60-80 seconds of work, not with double an EMOM. Use the extra time for heavier loads, a pair of movements, or a short run, but only one of those at a time. Set the timer as a single 2:00 interval repeated, add a warning before each window, and if the work keeps going past 90 seconds, step up to E3MOM.
