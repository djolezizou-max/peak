You picked four boulder problems and a plan that says four sets. The set is those four, climbed one after another. Then you rest. Then you do it again. By the second rest you are watching someone else's project, and when you pull on again you could not say whether that break was three minutes or ten.

The other mix-up is where the rest even goes. A bouldering 4x4 puts the four problems together and keeps the clock for the break between sets. It is a different session from the [Norwegian 4x4](/blog/posts/norwegian-4x4-interval-timer), which is four minutes hard and three minutes easy on a bike or a run. Here a problem ends when you top it or come off it, and the timer's job is the rest you would otherwise negotiate on the mat.

## Climb the four, then rest

The shape most gyms mean is the one in the name. [TrainingBeta's 4x4](https://www.trainingbeta.com/4x4s/) is four problems in a row, four times, with four minutes between the sets. They suggest problems about two to four grades under your max, and that the four should take roughly five or six minutes. That window is a check on the problems you chose. It is not a beep you have to hit before you are allowed to rest.

Other coaches keep the four-by-four and move the rest. [Uphill Athlete](https://uphillathlete.com/rock-climbing/bouldering-4-x-4-drills/) has you note how long the four problems took and rest twice that long. [Salt Pump](https://www.saltpumpclimbing.com/bouldering-4x4s-tips-tricks/) rests about as long as the set took, and treats "one problem, four times" as the same session as "four problems, four times through." What they share is the part that disappears in a busy gym: almost no rest inside the four, and a rest you decided before you started, once the four are done.

A timed sit-down after every problem is a different workout. The pump never gets a chance to accumulate, and the break between sets stops meaning anything because you already recovered under the wall. Pick the four, in order, before you start. A simple order is the steepest or hardest of them first, while you still have something left.

## Set the work phase as a ceiling

A fixed work interval is the wrong tool for a boulder problem. Too short and the rest starts while you are still on the third one. Too long and you stand under the last hold waiting for a beep that was never the point of the set.

Set one work phase longer than the four problems take you. Eight minutes is a practical ceiling if you are using that five-to-six-minute window as a guide: long enough that a slow walk between problems does not cut you off, short enough that a forgotten skip does not become a hangout. Set the rest to four minutes, or to the matched or doubled rest your plan actually uses. Set the rounds to four. Put fifteen seconds of prep in front so you can chalk up and walk to the first problem before the clock starts.

When you step off the fourth problem, skip ahead. The rest starts then, at the length you saved, instead of whenever you feel ready. If you forget, the ceiling ends the set for you. That nudge is the backup, not the goal. [An interval timer with a set counter](/blog/posts/interval-timer-with-set-counter) is the same skip used for barbell sets that end when the reps are done. Here the set is four problems.

In Peak Interval that is one interval with a work length, a rest length and a round count. Skipping from the work phase starts that set's rest immediately. Name the interval "4 problems" so the screen is not a blank countdown. The timer can tell you this is set three of four. It cannot tell you which of the four problems you meant, which is why that order lives on a note you made before you pulled on.

## Start the rest from the mat

The phone is on the edge of the pad, usually under a film of chalk. Unlocking it after a set is how four minutes becomes a conversation. Peak Interval puts the skip control on the lock-screen Live Activity, so ending the set is one tap through the glass while the phone stays where you put it.

The Apple Watch covers the other end of the rest. A haptic tap when the rest ends means you can shake out and look at the next problem instead of watching a phone on the mat. Pause and step back a phase both work from the wrist. Advancing into the rest from the Watch does not, yet, so the tap that closes the four problems stays on the phone. In a bouldering gym that is where your hands are anyway.

Give the start of the rest and the start of the next set different cues. "Rest" should sound nothing like "set three." Pumped, those are the only two facts you need, and you will not want to do the subtraction yourself. A trailing rest after set four, if your timer always pairs work with rest, is just you walking off the mat. The session you came for is already done.

## Keep the same rest on every set

Four minutes turns into ten because the gym is interesting and your forearms hurt. Resting until you feel ready makes the next session impossible to compare. If set three started after a long chat, you did not learn that the problems were too hard. You learned that you rested more.

Save the workout with the rest you chose. Next week, change the problems or the grade and leave the clock alone if you want to know what got harder. If your plan rests you for as long as the set took, or twice that, write the number down the first time and save that length. Deciding it again between every set is the drift, with a rule attached.

This is a different clock from [hangboard repeaters](/blog/posts/hangboard-repeaters-timer). Repeaters are seven seconds on and three seconds off, six times, with a long break you also put on the timer, because every second of the hang is the exercise. A 4x4 fails in the opposite place: the problems end when you finish them, and the break between the sets is the part that quietly grows.

## The short version

Pick four problems you can finish when you are fresh. Climb them back to back. Skip the timer into the rest you saved, and do that four times. Fifteen seconds of prep, a work ceiling around eight minutes, four-minute rests unless your plan says otherwise, and a cue that names the next set. You decide when the climbing is over. The timer decides when you get back on.
