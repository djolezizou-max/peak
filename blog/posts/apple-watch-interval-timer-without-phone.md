The whole reason you bought a watch was to stop carrying the phone. Then you actually leave it in the locker, start your session from the wrist, and find out which parts of your setup were quietly leaning on the thing in your pocket the entire time.

It is a bad moment to discover it. You are warm, the clock is running, and the workout you built last night either starts or it does not. So here is what genuinely runs standalone, what does not, and the short list worth checking before you walk away from the phone.

## What actually needs the phone, and what does not

The timing itself never needs the phone. Once an interval workout is on the Watch, the countdown, the phase changes and the haptic taps are all running on the Watch's own processor. Airplane mode, a locker two floors up, a phone that died overnight — none of it stops a session that has already started.

What does depend on the phone is everything that happens *before* the first rep. A workout you built on the iPhone has to have reached the Watch, which happens over the sync the two devices do when they are near each other. Build a new session on the train, leave the phone at home an hour later, and there is a real chance the Watch is still showing yesterday's list.

The third category is the one people get wrong most often: audio. If your cues come out of the phone's speaker or a pair of buds paired to the phone rather than the Watch, then leaving the phone behind does not silence the workout so much as remove your only signal. The haptics still fire. The beeps go with the phone.

## Three things to check before you leave it behind

**Open the workout on the Watch while the phone is still next to you.** Not the app — the actual workout. If it appears in the list on your wrist, it has synced and it will still be there in the gym. This takes five seconds and catches the single most common failure.

**Pair your headphones to the Watch, not the phone.** Bluetooth pairings are per-device, and a set of buds that connects instantly at home may be paired only to the iPhone. Do this once from the Watch's own Bluetooth settings and it stops being a question.

**Turn on Prominent Haptic.** In Sounds & Haptics on the Watch. Without the phone in play, the wrist tap is doing more work than usual, and the default strength is easy to miss at the bottom of a burpee. Tighten the band a notch while you are at it.

## Running Peak Interval standalone

Peak Interval's Watch app runs the session on the Watch. You build workouts on the iPhone, where a real keyboard and a real screen make setting up nested sets and per-phase cues quick, and those saved workouts live on the wrist afterwards. Starting one does not ask the phone for permission.

That means the prep phase, the work and rest blocks, the round counting and the distinct cue at each boundary all behave exactly as they do with the phone in your hand. The difference is which speaker the audio comes out of, and whether the session you built ten minutes ago has had a moment to make the trip.

If you train somewhere with no signal — a basement gym, a coastal path, a hotel room on airplane mode — this is the mode worth setting up deliberately rather than discovering by accident. Build the week's sessions on the phone on Sunday, glance at the Watch to confirm they landed, and the phone stops being part of the workout at all.

## When the phone is still worth carrying

Two cases, honestly. If you are doing heavy barbell work, a braced forearm masks the wrist tap, and a phone propped where you can see the phase change is a better primary signal with the haptic as backup. And if you want the session in Fitness with GPS for an outdoor run, the Watch handles that fine on its own, but you will want the phone if you also care about streaming music from it mid-session.

For most interval training, neither applies. The workout is on your wrist, the cues are on your wrist, and the phone was never doing anything except adding a thing to carry.

Set it up once with the phone beside you, confirm the workout is there, and then stop thinking about it.
