You sat down for calorie intervals: hard, easy, hard, easy, and a number on the console when you are done. The bike's own programs are not that session. They are a hill profile, a fat-burn ramp, or a preset whose work block is whatever the manufacturer chose, and changing it means more buttons while your legs are already warm.

A wall clock fails for the same reason. Your head is down, the fan is loud, and halfway through the set you are guessing whether the easy spin was thirty seconds or forty-five. [Peak Primal Wellness](https://peakprimalwellness.com/blogs/wellness/air-bike-workout-hiit-protocols-for-maximum-results) is direct about it: set the work and rest before you start, because watching a regular clock during a max-effort air-bike sprint is impractical.

## Score the calories, time the seconds

Air-bike pieces are often written in calories. That number is the score, not a clock you can set before you sit down. Calories only move while you are pushing, so a calorie target ends when you look down and see it, which is the moment you least want to look.

Give the two jobs to two devices. A timer owns the seconds. The console stays on calories, or watts, and you read what those seconds produced after the block. [Fit at Midlife's assault-bike workouts](https://fitatmidlife.com/28-awesome-assault-bike-workouts/) include the plain version: pedal hard for 30 seconds, easy or rest for 30 seconds, and repeat it 10 times. The same piece runs on an Assault or an Echo. What does not transfer is how short the hard side is allowed to be.

## An Echo sprint under 15 seconds is mostly spin-up

A spin-bike setup does not belong on this machine. On an upright bike, a Peloton, or a recumbent, the resistance is already there, so a 10 or 20 second sprint is real work. That is a different question, and it is the one answered in the [stationary bike HIIT workout](/blog/posts/stationary-bike-hiit-workout). A fan bike has to spin a fan up first, and an Echo takes longer than an Assault.

[The Workout Mag's Echo programming guide](https://theworkoutmag.com/training/rogue-echo-bike-periodization-hiit-programming) says the heavier belt-driven flywheel takes about 2 to 3 seconds longer to reach peak RPM than a chain-driven air bike. A 5-second sprint spends most of that interval overcoming the inertia. Their floor for anaerobic power intervals on the Echo is 15 seconds, so the fan is actually up to speed before the block ends. Their [Echo versus Assault comparison](https://theworkoutmag.com/equipment-workout/rogue-echo-bike-workouts-vs-assault-airbike) makes the other half of the point: the Assault AirBike Classic uses a lighter chain-driven fan that spins up faster, which is why a 10-second sprint can still be honest on that bike.

If you copied a 10-second sprint from a spin bike onto an Echo, lengthen the work to at least 15 seconds before you judge the calories. Shorter than that and you are mostly scoring the spin-up.

## A 30/30 you do not have to watch

Use the 30-on, 30-off piece from that assault-bike list, with a warm-up so the first sprint is not from a dead stop:

1. Five minutes of easy spinning, arms and legs together.
2. Ten seconds of prep to set your hands and get the fan turning before the first hard block.
3. Ten rounds of 30 seconds hard and 30 seconds easy. Easy is a light spin, not a full stop, unless you need the breath.
4. A few minutes easy to finish.

The interval block is 10 minutes. On an Echo, do not cut the hard side below 15 seconds. Fit at Midlife also lists 15 seconds on and 15 off. On an Echo that 15-second work side is the floor from the programming guide, so treat 15/15 as the short option and 30/30 as the one to learn. On an Assault, the faster spin-up means a 10-second sprint is available later. It is not the session to start with.

In the first week, stop at eight rounds. Add the last two once the tenth would still be a sprint. Write down the calories for the 10 minutes, or the calories on a single hard interval if the console shows them. Next week you beat that number at the same 30/30. You do not get there by shortening the rest until the fan never settles.

## Hear the change over the fan

Fit at Midlife's list says to put the phone in the holder on the bike and start. The comments under that page are people asking which holder that is, and whether it turns sideways. If your bike has no holder, the bench behind you is enough. You need to hear the change. You do not need to read a screen to know it happened.

A quiet beep disappears once the fan is at a sprint. A spoken "easy" a couple of seconds before the work ends lets you finish the push you are on and back off cleanly. A round count out loud, "round seven of ten", is what tells you to hold the back half instead of guessing which one you are in.

In Peak Interval you build this once: a prep phase so the fan is already moving, 30 seconds of work, 30 seconds of recovery, ten rounds, and a spoken cue on each change. Save the Echo workout with the work side no shorter than 15 seconds. Save a separate Assault workout if you later want shorter sprints on that bike. If the fan still covers the phone speaker, an Apple Watch taps your wrist at each change, and you can start the session from the wrist without reaching back to the bench.

## Seconds on the timer, calories on the console

Set the intervals before you sit down. Keep Echo work at 15 seconds or longer so the flywheel is actually up to speed, and use 30 seconds on and 30 off for ten rounds when you want one calorie piece that fits either bike. The console keeps the score. A timer you can hear keeps the clock.

Next week, change the calorie number at those same intervals, or add one round. Leave the 5-second sprint on the spin bike, where a short burst is actually work.
