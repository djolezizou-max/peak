The five minutes look like one block of work. They are five stations, a minute each, and the clock keeps running while you move. A rest beep in the middle of that block ends the station early and hands you a break the workout does not include.

By the third station every minute has sounded the same, so the box and the push press blur together. A for-time clock leaves that blur in place. The score is the reps you complete, and that clock never tells you when to move.

## What the 17 minutes are

[Wodball](https://www.wodball.com/en/wod/fight-gone-bad) writes Fight Gone Bad as three rounds of five stations: one minute of wall balls at 9/6 kg, one minute of sumo deadlift high-pulls at 34/25 kg, one minute of box jumps, one minute of push press at the same 34/25 kg, and one minute on the rower. One minute of rest sits between rounds. The stations run back to back. The sheet totals 17 minutes, and the score is movement reps plus rowing calories.

[Fit2Live](https://fit2livegym.com/fight-gone-bad/) runs that same clock: move as soon as the minute ends, and the clock does not reset or stop between exercises. Their list swaps the barbell high-pull for a kettlebell deadlift and high-pull at 16/24 kg. They score one point per rep, and each calorie on the rower is one point. The timer stays the same when the implement changes.

[Captime](https://captime.app/blog/fight-gone-bad-testing-your-limits-with-this-crossfit-classic/) describes the same shape: one minute at wall balls, sumo deadlift high-pulls, box jumps, push presses and the rower, no rest between them, one minute off, three rounds, 17 minutes. The [Rogue Home Timer manual](https://assets.roguefitness.com/image/upload/v1743771182/catalog/Gear%20and%20Accessories/Gym%20Essentials%20/Timers/IP0491/Home_Timer_User_manual-3-18-25_1_zw3cod.pdf) gives that clock its own mode. FGB is a 17-minute timer of three 5-minute rounds. Each round is five 1-minute intervals counting down with no rest between them, and two 1-minute rests sit between the rounds.

Three rounds of five minutes is 15 minutes of work. Two rests make 17. A rest after the third row makes 18, and the score was already finished when that row ended.

## Where a normal interval timer puts the beep

A work-and-rest pair wants a gap after every minute. Fight Gone Bad has that gap once, after the rower. Ten or fifteen seconds of transition between stations is a [circuit](/blog/posts/circuit-training-timer): a walk between stations, often of unequal length. These five stations are equal minutes, and the walk across the floor comes out of the next minute.

An [MMA round timer](/blog/posts/mma-round-timer) answers a different question. That clock is a long sport round, a rest, and a warning while you still have time to change what you are doing. Fight Gone Bad's five minutes are five jobs. A warning at the four-minute mark does not send you from the wall ball to the high-pull.

A for-time clock measures how long a fixed amount of work took. Fight Gone Bad has no fixed amount of work inside the minute. Each station stays open until the clock moves you, and the score is the total you completed. [For Time](/blog/posts/for-time-workout-meaning) is the format where the clock is the score. Here the session is 17 minutes for anyone who stays on the stations. The number that changes is the rep count.

One repeating minute with a single beep will move you on time and still leave the station count in your head. The minute you lose is the one where the high-pull and the push press use the same bar.

## Build the round once

In Peak Interval the round is five named intervals of 1:00 with nothing between them: Wall ball, High pull, Box jump, Push press, Row. Then one interval named Rest at 1:00. Set that group to repeat twice. That is two rounds and the two rests, 12 minutes. Append the same five stations once more, with no rest after the row. The workout is 17:00, and the last cue is the end of the third row, which is when you stop and add the score.

That nesting is the same shape as [repeating sets](/blog/posts/interval-timer-repeating-sets): build the group, append what comes after it, then save the whole thing. Keep the names to two words. That is what you will hear with a barbell in your hands. A preparation phase of about 15 seconds at the front gives you time to face the wall-ball target and put the phone down before the first minute starts. The Wodball bar and the Fit2Live kettlebell use those same five names.

If the builder attaches a rest to every round and the third round cannot stand on its own, run three rounds of the five stations plus rest and stop when the third row ends. The extra minute is a rest you leave on the floor. Waiting it out adds no reps.

## Hear which station you just left

The station change and the round rest need different cues. A single beep means something ended. You still have to remember whether you are walking to the box or sitting down. Name the intervals, and have the timer say the name. Row and Rest are the pair that matters: Row is still work, and Rest is the only minute the workout stops.

A spoken countdown at the end of each station puts you on the next one as the minute opens. The first reps of a new station are the ones that disappear when you notice the beep late.

The phone ends up at one end of the floor. The rower is at the other. Start the saved workout on Apple Watch when you have one, so the tap lands on your wrist when the room is loud and the phone speaker is behind you. Run one round at an easy effort before you load the bar. You should be able to name the station from the cue alone.

Use the rest minute to write the five numbers down. Wodball's pacing note is the useful one: the workout is scored by work completed, and emptying the tank on the wall balls leaves the other four stations short. Five numbers per round, three rounds, with the rower's calories included, is the whole score. Waiting until the end turns the first round into a guess.

## The takeaway

Set five stations at 1:00 with no gap, then 1:00 of rest, twice, and run the five stations once more. Name each station so the cue says where you are going. The clock is 17 minutes. The score is the reps, and the calories on the rower count with them.
