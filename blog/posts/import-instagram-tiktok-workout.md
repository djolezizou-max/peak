You open a workout on Instagram or TikTok, start moving, and then you are back in the app, scrubbing the reel to see what comes next. The set stops so you can read the caption. That pause is the whole problem. The point of the import is that you stop pausing the reel mid-workout.

Peak Interval turns that post into a timer, on iPhone and Android. For each move, a short loop of the creator's own video plays while you work. You can share the post in, or paste the link. A photo of a whiteboard is a different path, covered in [how to turn a workout screenshot into an interval timer](/blog/posts/import-workout-from-screenshot).

## Share the post, or paste the link

In Instagram or TikTok, tap Share on the workout post and choose Peak Intervals. A share-sheet import lands in Saved.

In the app, go to Import, then Instagram or TikTok post, paste the link, and tap Import Workout. An in-app import opens the editor with "Workout imported! Review and adjust as needed."

While it works, the progress steps read Finding the post, Watching the video, Building your workout.

## Which links work

Instagram post links, Reel links, and TV links all work, including carousels. The path in the URL can be /p, /reel, /reels, or /tv. On TikTok, video links and photo links work, including the short links with vm., vt., or /t/ in them. A YouTube link does not.

Carousels are supported, and so are TikTok photo posts. Paste the link to the post. The same Import step reads it.

## What it reads, and what it fills in

It reads the caption, the on-screen text, and the video. It understands circuits and rounds, work and rest, Tabata, EMOM, AMRAP, for time, reps including ranges, and "each side". How those formats run once they are on a timer is in the [Tabata](/blog/posts/how-to-use-a-tabata-timer), [EMOM](/blog/posts/how-to-run-an-emom-workout), [AMRAP](/blog/posts/how-to-run-an-amrap-workout), and [for time](/blog/posts/for-time-workout-meaning) guides. A session that mixes rep targets with timed blocks is the same problem as [timing reps and timed rounds together](/blog/posts/timer-for-reps-and-timed-rounds).

Numbers the post does not state are left out. They are not guessed. The gaps are filled with defaults of 30 seconds of work, 15 seconds of rest, and 60 seconds between rounds. The exception is a post that gives reps and no time. Then the work time is estimated from the pace in the video.

That is why the editor asks you to review. Look at the round count, the exercise names, and any time the caption never wrote down. A default is a starting point. It is not a claim that the creator programmed 30 seconds.

## What plays while you train

For each move, a loop of up to 8 seconds from the creator's video plays in the timer. The loop is muted, so your music and the app's cues stay in charge. If the playlist is swallowing those cues, the setup in [interval timer with music](/blog/posts/interval-timer-with-music) is the one that sorts the two audio streams. The words the timer speaks once the workout exists are a separate feature, in [interval timer with voice cues](/blog/posts/interval-timer-with-voice-coach).

During rest, a "Next up" badge appears with the next move's clip. The overlay shows the rep target, the exercise name, and an @handle credit. The handle links back to the original post.

Tap a move's work time when the loop is the wrong part of the video. You pick a different stretch, and the clip pauses and plays with the timer. Rest still shows the next move, so you can set up before the work starts.
