The Gibala sprint interval is a short all-out effort and a long easy recovery. About 30 seconds as hard as you can go, then about four minutes easy, repeated four to six times. A track version of the same pair goes as high as seven. Four minutes is long enough that the next sprint starts when you feel ready, which is early, or when you remember it, which is late.

You need two cues that mean different things. Sprint means 30 seconds all-out. Easy means stay easy until that cue comes back, about four minutes later. A Tabata timer runs 20 seconds on and 10 off. A beep every 30 seconds fires eight times during the recovery you are walking. This session needs the long gap to stay quiet, and one clear call when it ends.

## What the Gibala clock actually is

[Precision Nutrition](https://www.precisionnutrition.com/interval-training) describes the study from Martin Gibala's group at McMaster as about 20 minutes of intervals: a 30-second sprint followed by a four-minute rest, compared with 90 to 120 minutes of steady work in a target heart-rate zone.

A review of that low-volume work puts the usual session at four to six all-out cycling efforts of 30 seconds, with about four minutes of recovery between them. That is two to three minutes of hard work inside a session of about 20 minutes ([Gibala and colleagues, 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3381816/)). One comparison in the same literature used 4.5 minutes of easy pedaling at a low wattage between those 30-second efforts, still four to six times (Burgomaster and colleagues, 2008, listed in the protocol table of [MacInnis and Gibala, 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5407969/)). Four minutes is the number most people type in. When the write-up you are copying says 4:30, the timer should say 4:30.

The top end of "four to seven" is a track progression, with the same 30 seconds. [Koral and colleagues](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5839711/) had trained runners do 30-second all-out shuttle runs with 4 minutes of recovery, three times a week for two weeks. The bout count rose from 4 to 7 across the first five sessions and dropped back to 4 on the sixth. During the four minutes they walked back to the start line. Before the sprints they did light contractions, 5 minutes of easy aerobic work, and six short build-up runs.

[Time.tz](https://time.tz/tool/sprint-timer/) ships that pair as a preset called Classic SIT: 30 seconds all-out, 4 minutes of recovery.

The [Norwegian 4x4](/blog/posts/norwegian-4x4-interval-timer) is the neighbouring protocol, and the blocks are reversed: four minutes hard, three minutes easy, four times. Programming that timer and treating the opening 30 seconds as the sprint leaves you inside a four-minute effort.

## A Tabata preset and a 30-second beep are different sessions

Tabata is 20 seconds on and 10 seconds off. A [Tabata timer](/blog/posts/best-tabata-timer) is built so that 20/10 stays obvious, including across a classic block of eight rounds. Eight rounds is four minutes, and the rest is a third of the work. A Gibala session spreads about two to three minutes of sprinting across roughly 20 minutes, and the rest is the long piece.

A [30-second interval timer](/blog/posts/30-second-interval-timer) is a cue every 30 seconds. Use it when every boundary means "switch." Here the recovery is eight of those slices, and the sprint is only the first one. The beep keeps arriving while you walk, and one of those arrivals becomes the moment you go again.

The session wants a sprint label of 0:30 and an easy label of 4:00. Easy should stay quiet enough to walk, and it should be unmistakable at the end.

## Build 0:30 and 4:00, then choose how many

In Peak Interval the pair is two named intervals: Sprint at 0:30, Easy at 4:00. Set the repeat to 4. That is where both the cycling session (four to six) and the Koral progression (they began at four) start. Save it.

When the last sprint is still all-out, duplicate the workout and raise the repeat. Six is the top of the usual Wingate session. Seven is the top of the Koral ramp, and that protocol brought the last session back to four, so seven is a peak day. When your source uses 4:30 of easy pedaling, change Easy to 4:30 and leave Sprint at 0:30.

Put the warm-up in the same saved workout. Koral's runners took 5 minutes easy, plus short build-ups, before the first sprint. Five minutes named Easy in front of the repeats keeps the first 0:30 as a sprint. A preparation phase of about 15 seconds at the front is enough to pocket the phone before that easy five minutes starts.

A repeating pair also places an Easy block after the last sprint. Leave it. The extra four minutes is the walk back to the line. Deleting it only on the last round makes the last repeat a different workout.

Save two workouts:

1. **Four.** 15 seconds of prep, 5:00 Easy, then Sprint 0:30 / Easy 4:00 repeated four times.
2. **Six.** The same warm-up, with the repeat set to 6. Duplicate it and set the repeat to 7 only on a day you have already handled six.

## Let the recovery call the next sprint

Have the timer say the name. Sprint and Easy are different instructions, and a single beep at both ends still leaves you to remember which one just started. After four minutes of walking, that memory is the part that slips. Two words is as much as you will catch, and it is enough.

A spoken countdown at the end of Easy is what keeps the four minutes honest. You hear the sprint coming, so the start lands on 4:00. Going at three minutes because your legs feel fresh, or missing the start because you were looking down the straight, are the two ways a long recovery drifts. Koral's runners were told to keep each 30 seconds maximal, without pacing. Working out the time left in your head is how a maximal sprint becomes a measured one.

On an Apple Watch, start the saved workout from the wrist. The tap at each change still lands when wind or a bike fan covers the phone, and the watch face does not have to be readable. Run one recovery at home first. Lock the phone, put it in the pocket you will use, and check that you hear Easy at the start and Sprint only when the four minutes are done.

## The takeaway

Set Sprint to 0:30 and Easy to 4:00. Put five minutes easy in front, repeat the pair four times, and save six as the next step. The next sprint starts when the timer says Sprint.
