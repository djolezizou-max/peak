The rules fit in one line: one burpee in minute one, two in minute two, three in minute three, and you keep going until you cannot finish the minute's reps before the next one starts. Then you stop. That is the whole workout, and it is why everyone remembers their first one.

What nobody tells you is that the hard part is not the burpees, it is the clock. By minute twelve you are too cooked to remember whether this is minute twelve or thirteen, and the number of the minute is the number of reps you owe. This guide covers how the format actually plays out, how to set the timer so it does the counting, how to score a minute you failed, and how to run the same structure with other movements.

## How it plays out minute by minute

Death by burpees is an EMOM where the work grows and the window does not. The first five minutes feel like a warm-up: three or four burpees take ten seconds and you rest the other fifty. The total by the end of minute ten is still only 55 burpees.

Then the arithmetic turns on you. A steady burpee takes about three seconds, so 12 burpees take roughly 36 seconds and leave you 24 to recover. At 15 you are working for 45 seconds and resting 15. At 18 you have six seconds, which is not rest at all. Most people's last finished minute lands somewhere in the low-to-mid teens; getting past 20 means holding three-second burpees with almost no break, which is a very fit athlete's score.

That curve is why the format is also a test. The first half barely registers, the last three or four minutes are a near all-out effort, and where you stop says a lot about your burpee pace and how well you hold it under fatigue. Treat it as a benchmark you repeat every six to eight weeks rather than a weekly workout.

## Set the clock so it counts for you

Underneath, death by burpees is one 1:00 interval repeated with no rest phase, because the rest is whatever the minute leaves. Set more rounds than you will ever reach, 30 is plenty, and end it when you fail. You never need a work and rest split.

The thing that matters is the round number. Since round number equals rep target, a timer that shows "round 14" on screen is telling you that you owe 14 burpees, and you no longer have to keep that count in your head while also counting reps. A countdown beep or a 3-second warning before each minute also helps: in the late rounds you want to be standing over the mat at 0:00, not walking back to it.

## Scoring a failed minute

The score is the last minute you finished, plus the reps you got in the minute you failed. If you complete minute 14 and manage 9 of the 15 burpees in minute 15, your score is 14 + 9. Writing both parts tells you more next time than "14" alone, because 14 + 2 and 14 + 13 are very different days.

Decide your standard before you start and keep it the same every time: chest and thighs touching the floor, a small jump, hands above the head. Lazy reps in minute 15 buy you a round but break the comparison with your last attempt. Some gyms also let you keep going for one more minute after a miss, scoring the total, but if you are tracking progress, stopping at the first miss is the cleaner rule.

## The same format with other movements

Any movement you can do quickly and safely while tired works: death by thrusters with a light barbell, death by pull-ups, death by kettlebell swings, death by wall balls. The timer setup is identical, a single 1:00 interval repeated, only the name of the interval changes.

Pick the load so that a single rep takes two to four seconds. Heavier than that and the workout ends at minute eight on strength, not conditioning, which is a different test. Pull-ups end sooner for most people because grip fails before breathing does, so expect a lower score and don't compare it with your burpee number.

You can also start higher to skip the easy part. Starting at 5 burpees in minute one, for example, keeps the same finish but cuts the first few minutes that barely count. Just record the starting number next to the score so you are comparing like with like.

## The short version

Death by burpees adds one rep a minute until you miss. Set a single 1:00 interval with more rounds than you will reach, let the round number be your rep target, and add a warning before each minute. Score it as last finished minute plus reps in the failed one, keep the rep standard strict, and run it as a benchmark every couple of months rather than a regular workout.
