You are into the 15-second reps and you cannot tell whether this one is the third or the fourth. The float that was supposed to stay quick has slowed to a walk, because nothing told you the recovery was still on the clock.

The Mona fartlek is twenty minutes of four effort lengths. Two efforts of 90 seconds, then four of 60, four of 30 and four of 15. Each effort is followed by a float of the same length. A single repeating interval holds one pair. This run changes the pair four times.

## Twenty minutes, four lengths

Runner's World UK, The Morning Shakeout and Runlovers give the same script: 2 x 90 seconds hard with a 90-second float, then 4 x 60, 4 x 30 and 4 x 15. Each float matches the effort in front of it.

Add the pairs. Two times 90 plus 90 is 6 minutes. Four times 60 plus 60 is 8 minutes. Four times 30 plus 30 is 4 minutes. Four times 15 plus 15 is 2 minutes. That is 20 minutes, and it stays 20 while every float runs as long as the effort it follows.

Runlovers says to set a timer or memorize the sequence, because the 15-second reps leave no time to think. Memorizing carries the 90s. It runs out when the efforts are 15 seconds and you are also trying to remember the count.

## A repeating pair stays one length

An interval timer usually asks for work, rest and a round count. That builds equal hard and easy repeats, which is a different run: an [interval running workout](/blog/posts/interval-running-workout-timer). Both sides are chosen before you start, and they stay chosen. Set 90 and 90 and repeat it, and the session stays at 90. The 60s, the 30s and the 15s are a different block.

A [random interval timer](/blog/posts/random-interval-timer) answers a different question. Those cues are for a fartlek you are not supposed to be able to predict. The Mona is a fixed order. You need to hear which length you are in, and you need to hear the float end while it is still quick.

The [10-20-30 run](/blog/posts/10-20-30-running-timer) is different again. Three paces live inside one repeating minute. Here the efforts get shorter across the twenty minutes, and the float copies whichever length you just ran.

## Four pairs in one saved workout

In Peak Interval each length is its own pair, with the repeat on that pair.

1. **90.** Hard at 1:30 and Float at 1:30, repeated 2 times.
2. **60.** Hard at 1:00 and Float at 1:00, repeated 4 times.
3. **30.** Hard at 0:30 and Float at 0:30, repeated 4 times.
4. **15.** Hard at 0:15 and Float at 0:15, repeated 4 times.

That is the same shape as [repeating sets](/blog/posts/interval-timer-repeating-sets): build the pair, set its repeat, then append the next pair. Fourteen efforts and fourteen floats, entered as eight intervals. Change the 60-second float and it changes for all four, because there is only one of them.

Name the efforts 90, 60, 30 and 15, and name every recovery Float. Have the timer say the name. "15" means the short effort. "Float" means stay quick until you hear the next effort. When the block changes you hear 60 instead of 90, then 30, then 15. The same beep for every boundary leaves you working out which side you are on while the 15 seconds disappear.

A preparation phase of about 15 seconds at the front is enough to pocket the phone before the first 90. A spoken countdown in the last seconds of the float puts you into the next effort as it starts. On the 15s, a late cue eats the rep.

Easy running before or after the twenty minutes belongs in the same saved workout. A second timer between blocks is where the float turns into a walk.

## Hear the length, and keep the float moving

The 15-second block is two minutes: four efforts, four floats, eight cues. The repeat of four is the tally. The cue only has to say which side of the pair you are on.

The float has its own sound, and it ends on its own seconds. A float you finish when you feel ready grows each time the efforts get shorter, and the twenty minutes stops being the script you set.

Start the saved workout on an Apple Watch. The tap at each change still lands with the phone in a pocket, and the watch face does not have to be readable for you to know whether this is the effort or the float. Before the first real session, run the 30s and the 15s at home with the phone locked in the pocket you will use. You should hear the length change, and you should hear each float end when its seconds are done.

## The takeaway

Two 90s, four 60s, four 30s and four 15s, each with a float of the same length. Build those four pairs, have the timer say the length, and keep the float quick until the next cue. That is the twenty minutes. Memorizing the sequence is the other option, and the 15-second reps are where it runs out of time.
