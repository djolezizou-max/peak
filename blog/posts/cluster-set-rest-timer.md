You rack the bar after four reps and start counting to twenty. It is never twenty. The first pause is close. By the last mini-set you have stood there until the bar felt possible again, and the cluster has turned into one slow set of twelve.

A rest timer between sets starts after that whole cluster. The short break inside it, the one that makes these mini-sets a cluster, stays a count in your head. You need both clocks on the same workout: 15–20 seconds between mini-sets, then the normal multi-minute rest after the cluster.

## Two rests, and they do different jobs

Muscle & Fitness programs hypertrophy clusters as 4+4+4, with 15–20 seconds between those mini-sets, or 20–30 seconds when the setup is heavier. Sci-Fit puts 15–45 seconds between sub-sets, then a normal 2–4 minute rest once the cluster is done. The Barbell describes that same 15–20 second break inside the set, the one lifters otherwise count in their heads.

The short rest is part of the set. It is long enough to breathe and reset your grip, and short enough that the next four reps are still the same effort. The long rest is the break between clusters. A [rest timer between sets](/blog/posts/rest-timer-between-sets) already runs that one: two to four minutes so the next cluster can start clean.

An [EMOM](/blog/posts/emom-meaning) is a different window. The minute opens, you do the reps, and the rest is whatever the minute leaves. A cluster waits 15–20 seconds between mini-sets. The multi-minute rest starts only after the cluster.

## The twenty seconds is the part that stretches

The first intra-set break is easy to hit. You are fresh, you rack the bar, you count, you lift. The third one is where the count grows, because you stay at the hooks until the next four feel reasonable, and reasonable arrives later when the reps were heavy. That stretch is the cluster leaving. The mini-sets were supposed to stay heavy. Extra seconds turn them into a long set with pauses you did not write down.

Stay standing. The short rest is a pause at the bar, chalk still on your hands, rather than a seat on the bench. Sitting down is how twenty seconds becomes the long rest, early, in the middle of the cluster.

A countdown replaces the count. You rack the fourth rep, the twenty seconds runs, and a cue tells you to lift. The wall clock stays out of it.

## Build the short rest inside the cluster

The reps are not timed. Four reps take as long as four reps take. The timer owns the pause after them, and the pause after the cluster.

Set the work phase as a ceiling, the same way an [interval timer with a set counter](/blog/posts/interval-timer-with-set-counter) does for a strength set that ends when the reps are done. Sixty seconds is longer than four reps should take, and short enough that a forgotten skip does not turn the pause into a second rest. Set the rest to 20 seconds. Repeat that pair three times. That is the 4+4+4. Then add the cluster rest, anywhere in the 2–4 minute range, and repeat the whole cluster for the number of sets in the session. [Repeating sets](/blog/posts/interval-timer-repeating-sets) are what let that inner pair sit inside the outer rest.

In Peak Interval that is one saved workout:

1. Prepare for 15 seconds, long enough to chalk up and set your feet.
2. A mini-set with a 60-second ceiling. Skip forward when the fourth rep is racked, so the short rest starts then. If the ceiling arrives first, the rest starts on its own.
3. A 20-second rest.
4. That mini-set and short rest, three times.
5. A cluster rest of 3 minutes, or 2 minutes when you want the short end of the 2–4 minute range.
6. An outer repeat for the number of clusters.

A repeating pair still places a 20-second rest after the last mini-set, and the cluster rest follows it. You will hear the short-rest cue once more, then the long one. That last 20 seconds is the start of the break between clusters. The pauses that have to stay at 20 seconds are the two between the mini-sets. A saved cluster rest of 2:00, 3:00, or 3:40, with that final 20 seconds in front, lands on 2:20, 3:20, or 4:00. All three sit in the 2–4 minute rest Sci-Fit puts after the cluster.

Heavier work uses the same shape. Muscle & Fitness lengthens the intra-set pause to 20–30 seconds on heavier setups. Change 20 to 30 and leave the cluster rest in that 2–4 minute range. A plan that says 15 seconds, or 45, is the same workout with the short rest edited.

Name the phases so the cue tells you which rest you are in. "Reps" and "breath" are enough. One word is what you will hear with your hands on the bar. The same beep at both ends still leaves you to remember whether this pause is twenty seconds or three minutes.

Give the short rest and the cluster rest different sounds. After three minutes you need to hear that the next mini-set opens a new cluster. ## The takeaway

Program 4+4+4 with 20 seconds between mini-sets, or 30 seconds when the setup is heavier, then 2–4 minutes after the cluster. Skip into the short rest when you rack the bar. Let the timer call the long rest on its own. The twenty seconds stays twenty, and the cluster stays a cluster.
