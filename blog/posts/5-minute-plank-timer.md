You set five minutes on a stopwatch, drop into a front plank, and spend the first minute looking at the phone on the mat. Every glance pulls your neck up and your hips down, so the hold you are counting is not the hold you think you are doing.

Five minutes in one shape is also a different session from five minutes of planks. The version you can finish, and the one you do not have to remember, is a series: front, side and reverse, with the timer calling each switch. You hold the position. The clock is no longer something you watch.

## Build five minutes as five named holds

Five intervals of 60 seconds is 5:00. Put them in an order that makes the next side obvious:

- Front plank, 60 seconds
- Side plank, right, 60 seconds
- Front plank, 60 seconds
- Side plank, left, 60 seconds
- Reverse plank, 60 seconds

The second front plank is doing a job. Rolling from the right side straight to the left side is how the wrong arm ends up on the mat. A minute back on the front gives the shoulders a different position and makes "left" the next clear instruction instead of a guess.

Add 10 seconds of preparation before the first hold. That is long enough to set your forearm or your hand, put the phone face down, and be still when the work starts. If the prep and the first plank are the same moment, the five minutes begin while you are still arranging yourself, and the opening seconds are not a plank.

## Let the cue say which side is next

Both side planks feel like "the side one" once you are tired. A timer that beeps the same beep at every minute tells you that time is up. It does not tell you which way to roll, so you reconstruct the order from memory, which is the thing you were trying not to do.

Name each interval after the position: "Front plank", "Side, right", "Front plank", "Side, left", "Reverse plank". Two words is enough. You are not reading a sentence from a forearm plank, and on a watch the countdown already takes most of the face. The guide to an [interval timer that shows exercise names](/blog/posts/interval-timer-that-shows-exercise-names) is the same rule: the label answers what is happening now, and it has to survive a glance.

The cue at the switch should say that name. A spoken "side, left" at the boundary is the reason you can keep your head down. Give that switch a different sound from the end of the workout. Five identical beeps and you will count them anyway, which puts you back on the stopwatch.

## Shorten the hold when a full minute breaks the line

A 60-second side plank is a real hold. If your hips drop before the minute is up, do not turn the timer off and guess the rest. Keep the session at five minutes and spend part of each minute getting into the next position. The time you spend holding goes down. That is what the rest split is for.

Use 40 seconds of hold and 20 seconds to switch, in the same order:

- Front plank, 40 seconds
- To side, right, 20 seconds
- Side plank, right, 40 seconds
- To front, 20 seconds
- Front plank, 40 seconds
- To side, left, 20 seconds
- Side plank, left, 40 seconds
- To reverse, 20 seconds
- Reverse plank, 40 seconds
- Done, 20 seconds

Forty plus twenty, five times, is 300 seconds. The switch intervals are named after where you are going, so the rest tells you the next position. Twenty seconds is enough to roll, set the hand, and find a straight line before the next hold. The last 20 seconds is the end of the session, not another plank. You come down from the reverse plank and the timer stops.

If 40 seconds still breaks the position, use 30 seconds on and 30 seconds to set up, same five positions. Each pair is still 60 seconds, and five of them are still 5:00. Change the two numbers. Keep the sum.

Do not collapse this into one interval repeated five times. A repeat only works when every pass is the same work, which is the setup in [repeating sets](/blog/posts/interval-timer-repeating-sets). Here the names change on purpose. Five pairs, one saved workout, in the order above.

## Leave the phone where you cannot read it

The cheat is the glance. You look to see what is left, the neck cranks, the hips sag, and you count that as the hold. Face down, the phone cannot help you do it.

Start the workout and put the phone where the speaker still reaches you and the screen does not. Across the room is fine if you can hear the cue. On the mat, face down, is fine if you trust it. Either way you are not negotiating with a number.

On Apple Watch the same workout runs from the wrist. A haptic tap at each switch tells you the position changed without a look at the phone. The name of the next hold is the part you needed. The remaining seconds are optional.

## Run the five minutes without watching them

Five holds, 60 seconds each: front, side right, front, side left, reverse. Ten seconds of prep so the first plank starts already set. Each interval named, and a cue at the switch that says the name.

If a full minute breaks your line, make each position 40 seconds and give the next 20 seconds to getting there, and keep the total at five minutes. Then put the phone where you cannot watch it. The timer has the clock and the next position. You have the hold.
