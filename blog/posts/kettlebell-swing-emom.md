You decided to do 100 kettlebell swings. The first 40 go fine. Somewhere around 60 your hips stop snapping, your arms start lifting the bell, and you take a long, unplanned break with your hands on your knees before grinding out the last sets with form you would not want filmed.

The swings were not the problem. The rest was. Without a structure, you either rush it because you want to be done, or you stretch it because you are tired, and both wreck the back half of the session. An EMOM fixes that by deciding the rest for you.

## Why an EMOM suits a fixed swing count

EMOM means every minute on the minute: you start a set at the top of each minute and rest for whatever is left. For swings that is close to ideal. A swing set is short and explosive, the quality drops fast once you are fatigued, and the main thing that keeps quality high is consistent rest between sets.

The simplest version is 10 swings every minute for 10 minutes. That is 100 swings in exactly 10 minutes. Ten good two-handed swings take roughly 15 to 20 seconds, which leaves 40 seconds or more to breathe before the next minute starts. You finish the session having done every set fresh enough to keep your hips doing the work.

Compare that with "100 swings for time." The total is the same, but most people front-load big sets, hit a wall, and spend the last third doing broken sets of five. The EMOM spreads the effort evenly, which is exactly what you want if the goal is good swings rather than a fast number.

## Choosing reps per minute and the bell

Pick the bell first. It should be heavy enough that set ten feels like work, but light enough that rep ten of each set looks like rep one. For most people starting out that is 12 to 16 kg for women and 16 to 24 kg for men. If you are unsure, go lighter; the EMOM will tell you within three minutes whether you need more.

Then match the reps to how much rest you want:

1. **10 swings, 10 minutes (100 total):** the standard. About 40 seconds of rest per minute. Start here.
2. **15 swings, 10 minutes (150 total):** about 30 seconds of rest. A good step once 10 feels easy.
3. **20 swings, 10 minutes (200 total):** 15 to 20 seconds of rest. This is a conditioning session, not a technique session.
4. **10 swings, 20 minutes (200 total):** same volume as the previous line but far more rest, so you can use a heavier bell.

If you only have one bell and the 100 gets easy, raise the reps per minute before you add minutes. Ten more minutes of easy swings builds patience more than fitness.

## Setting it up on a timer

On an interval timer, an EMOM is one phase of 60 seconds repeated for as many rounds as you want. For the standard session that is a single 1:00 phase, 10 rounds. Do not build ten separate entries; one repeating phase with a round counter is less to set up and tells you exactly which minute you are on.

Add a 15 to 20 second prep phase before the first round. That is enough time to set your feet, hike the bell back, and start the first swing on the cue instead of chasing the clock from second one. A ten-second warning before each new minute helps too: you hear it, you step up to the bell, and every set starts on time.

## Variations that keep the same timer

Once the basic EMOM is set up, a few changes cover most of what you would want from swings:

- **Alternating minutes:** two 1:00 phases, "two-handed swings" and "goblet squats" or "push-ups," repeated 5 or 10 times. Naming each phase means the cue tells you what is next, so you never have to work out whether minute seven is odd or even.
- **One-arm swings:** alternate left and right each minute, 8 to 10 reps per arm. Same two-phase setup, named "left" and "right."
- **Ladder:** 5 swings in minute one, 10 in minute two, 15 in minute three, then back to 5. Use three phases with different names and repeat the block three or four times.

If you start missing the minute, meaning your set runs past the next cue, drop two reps per set for the rest of the session. An EMOM you finish cleanly is worth more than one you chase.

## The practical takeaway

For a fixed swing count, let the clock pace the sets. Start with 10 swings every minute for 10 minutes, keep the rest as rest, and only raise the reps per minute when the last set looks as sharp as the first. Set it up once as a single repeating minute with a prep countdown, and the 100 stops being a grind you survive and becomes a session you can repeat three times a week.
