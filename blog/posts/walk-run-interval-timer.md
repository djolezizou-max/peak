The plan says run one minute, walk two, repeat eight times. Simple on paper. Then you are on the pavement, slightly out of breath, trying to remember whether that was walk four or walk five, and whether the run started when you passed the lamp post or the bin.

Walk-run is the gentlest way into running, but it asks you to do arithmetic at exactly the moment you least want to. And every week the plan changes the ratio, so whatever you set up last week is already wrong. The fix is to stop treating it as one timer and start treating it as a short series of saved timers, one per week, each of which you only have to follow.

## Why walk-run works, and why counting ruins it

The method is old and well tested: alternate short running spells with walking recoveries, and shift the balance towards running over a few weeks. Your heart, lungs and legs get repeated exposure to running without the single long effort that makes new runners stop, ache or quit. Most beginners who follow it can run 20 to 30 minutes continuously in eight to ten weeks.

What derails it is rarely fitness. It is drift. Walks stretch because they feel good, runs get cut short because they do not, and losing count means you either do six rounds instead of eight or add a ninth to be safe. A cue at every change removes all of that. You run when it says run and walk when it says walk.

## A nine-week ratio ladder you can save as nine timers

Each session below starts with a 5-minute brisk walk and ends with a 5-minute easy walk. Run at a pace where you could still speak in short sentences. Do three sessions a week, and repeat a week if it felt like a struggle rather than moving up.

| Week | Run | Walk | Rounds | Running time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1:00 | 2:00 | 8 | 8 min |
| 2 | 1:30 | 2:00 | 7 | 10.5 min |
| 3 | 2:00 | 2:00 | 6 | 12 min |
| 4 | 3:00 | 1:30 | 5 | 15 min |
| 5 | 4:00 | 1:30 | 4 | 16 min |
| 6 | 5:00 | 1:00 | 4 | 20 min |
| 7 | 8:00 | 1:00 | 3 | 24 min |
| 8 | 12:00 | 1:00 | 2 | 24 min |
| 9 | 25:00 | — | 1 | 25 min |

Notice the shape. The early weeks lengthen the run while the walk stays put; from week 4 the walk starts to shrink as well. Total session length stays between about 30 and 40 minutes throughout, so the plan never asks for more time, only a different split of it.

## Set each week up once, before you need it

Build all nine on a quiet evening rather than on the morning of the run. Name them by week ("Walk-Run W1 1:2", "Walk-Run W2 1.5:2") so the right one is obvious at a glance, and include the warm-up and cool-down walks inside each timer instead of timing them separately. If they are not in the timer, they tend not to happen.

In Peak Interval that means a short preparation phase (15 seconds is enough to get the phone into a pocket), a 5-minute warm-up block, the run and walk pair repeated for that week's rounds, and a 5-minute cool-down. Run, walk and preparation each get their own sound, so you can tell "speed up" from "slow down" without looking. A round counter tells you how many are left if you do glance, which helps more than you would think in the last two rounds. If you wear an Apple Watch, the same saved workout runs from your wrist with a tap at each change.

## Make the cues audible on a real route

Before week 1, do a two-minute test: start music or a podcast, lock the phone, put it in the pocket you will actually use and walk around the block. You should hear the cue over traffic and tell the run sound from the walk sound with your eyes closed. If you are on Android, check battery saver will not pause the timer with the screen off; [interval timer app for Android](/blog/posts/interval-timer-app-android) covers what to test.

Once you can run 20 minutes or more, the walk-run ladder has done its job. If you want to keep using intervals to get faster rather than to build up to running, [interval running workout timer](/blog/posts/interval-running-workout-timer) has hard-repeat sessions built the same way.

## The takeaway

Pick your starting week honestly, build that week and the next two as saved timers tonight, and add the rest as you get there. Repeat a week if you need to; nobody is grading the calendar. Your only job on the pavement is to run when you hear run and walk when you hear walk. The counting was never supposed to be yours.
