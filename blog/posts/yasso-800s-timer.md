You set the jog to three minutes, ran the 800 in 3:20, and the next one started while you were still bent over. The session on paper was ten 800s. The session on the track was ten 800s with a rest that belonged to a faster rep.

The jog is supposed to last as long as the 800 you just finished. You do not have that number until the two laps are done. A work and rest pair filled in before the start goes wrong the moment the split moves.

## Ten 800s, and a jog that copies that 800

Runner's World and Runstreet describe Yasso 800s as ten 800-meter repeats, with a recovery equal to the time of the 800. Middle Age Marathoner puts the same rule on the gap between them: the rest matches the length of that 800. On a 400-meter track, each 800 is two laps. The recovery is a jog, and it takes as long as those two laps took.

A 3:20 800 gets a 3:20 jog. A 3:28 800 gets a 3:28 jog. The next 800 starts when that jog ends. It does not start when you feel recovered, and it does not start when a rest you typed at home runs out.

## A preset pair starts the next 800 early or late

An interval timer usually wants both sides before you press start. That fits a run you designed as a warm-up, equal hard and easy repeats, and a cool-down: an [interval running workout](/blog/posts/interval-running-workout-timer). Both of those sides were chosen in advance, and they stay chosen.

Yasso copies the second number from the first, and the first number is a result. Type the jog as 3:00 and run the 800 in 3:20, and the next rep starts 20 seconds early. Type the jog as 3:30 and run 3:10, and you are still jogging after the recovery the session asked for. The ten reps no longer start from the same kind of rest.

Yasso sets the jog equal to the 800 you just ran. An [EMOM](/blog/posts/emom-meaning) is a different question: the rest is whatever is left of a fixed minute. A [swim send-off](/blog/posts/swim-interval-timer) is different again: you leave on a time you wrote down, and the rest is whatever the swim did not use.

## Set both sides when you have already chosen the time

Both boxes can be filled in before you leave on one kind of day. You have decided the 800s are a time, and you are going to run the two laps so they finish on that beep. The jog is the same number, because the 800 is that number if you hit it.

Take 3:20. The interval named 800 is 3:20. The interval named Jog is 3:20. Repeat that pair 10 times. You are running the two laps so they finish on the beep. The jog matches the 800 when the second lap ends as the 800 ends. Finish the laps at 3:10 and the beep is still coming: jog then and the recovery is shorter than the two laps, wait for the beep and the recovery is longer than the two laps. If the beep starts the jog before the second lap is done, the recovery is already short.

In Peak Interval that is one saved workout. A preparation phase of about 15 seconds is enough to pocket the phone before the first 800. Then 800 at 3:20 and Jog at 3:20, repeated 10. Have the timer say the name. "800" means run the two laps. "Jog" means stay jogging until you hear 800 again. The same beep for both leaves you working out which side you are on by the fourth rep. A spoken countdown in the last seconds of the jog puts you on the line as the next 800 begins.

A repeating pair also places a jog after the tenth 800. The protocol's jog sits between the reps, so that last one is extra easy running. Leave it. Save the time you are actually running, and when the 800s move to 3:15, duplicate the workout and change both sides. A 3:00 jog behind a 3:20 800 is the session that started the next rep early.

Start the saved workout on an Apple Watch. The tap at each change still lands in the wind, and the watch face does not have to be readable for you to know whether this is the 800 or the jog. Before the first real session, run one pair with the phone locked in the pocket you will use. You should hear 800, then a jog of the same length, and you should hear the next 800 only when that jog is finished.

## If the split decides the jog, leave it blank

Some days the 800 is whatever the two laps take, and the jog has to copy that split. You cannot type it before the first rep. A timer that asks for both sides before it will start is asking for a number that does not exist yet.

The usual fix is a running clock. The 800 ends at 3:20, you add 3:20, and the next one starts at 6:40. That 800 takes 3:28 and ends at 10:08, so the one after it starts at 13:36. You do that addition while you jog, and the rep count is what you drop. By the sixth you are no longer sure whether the next 800 is number seven.

A countdown of the split removes the addition. The two laps end, you start a rest of that exact length, and the count of ten lives somewhere other than the arithmetic. Peak Interval runs the durations you saved before the start. The saved workout is for the day you chose the time. On the day the split is in charge, write the time down when the two laps end, count that time down, and keep the rep number where you can see it.

## The takeaway

Ten 800s, and a jog equal to the 800 you just ran. If you have chosen that time, set both sides to it, repeat the pair ten times, and let the timer say which side you are in. If the two laps are going to take whatever they take, do not type the jog until the split exists. A rest filled in first starts the next rep early or late.
