You are halfway through the session you built so you would not have to think, and the watch is already in the red. The rounds that are left are still on the timer. The battery is not.

The countdown is the small part. What stays powered around it is the display, sound coming out of the watch itself, and a connection looking for your phone after you left it in a locker. Apple documents each of those in its [battery guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/122789) and its [Low Power Mode guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108320). Keeping a long timer alive is a settings pass.

## What stays awake during the session

An interval timer needs a clock and a way to mark the phase change. It does not need the screen held up for every second of the circuit.

Always On keeps a dim version of the current screen between glances. Apple lists turning it off as its own step, separate from any other saver: Settings, Display & Brightness, Always On. With it off, you tap the display when you want to see the time. Low Power Mode turns Always On off as well.

Newer Apple Watch models can play cues through the built-in speaker. Apple says that drains the battery more quickly than AirPods or another Bluetooth headset. A beep from the speaker on every interval is the expensive way to mark a boundary you could feel on your wrist.

## Turn Low Power Mode on yourself

Press the side button, tap the battery percentage, and turn Low Power Mode on. Settings, then Battery, is the other path. A yellow ring at the top of the screen means it is on. Apple turns it off again once the watch charges to 80 percent, unless you picked On for 1 Day, 2 Days, or 3 Days.

While it is on, Apple turns off Always On, background heart rate readings, heart rate notifications for an irregular rhythm and for a high or low heart rate, background blood oxygen, double tap and wrist flick, the start-workout reminder, and Time in Daylight. If this session depends on a high-heart-rate alert, leave Low Power Mode off. A circuit that only needs the next phase does not use that alert.

One switch looks like the right one and is not. Settings, Workout, Low Power Mode turns the mode on by itself when a workout starts in Apple's Workout app. Starting a timer in Peak Interval does not trip that switch. Turn Low Power Mode on before you press start.

Fewer GPS and Heart Rate Readings is narrower again. Apple applies it only to an outdoor walk, run, or hike in the Workout app, only while Low Power Mode is already on, and only on Apple Watch SE (2nd generation), Series 8 and later, and Ultra and later, with watchOS 9.1 or later. While it is active, workout alerts, splits, and current pace are off. It does not run for a gym circuit in a timer app.

When you do use the Workout app in Low Power Mode, Apple says heart rate and pace are still measured and the battery lasts longer. That is the tool for a logged outdoor run. The choice between that log and a dedicated timer is in [Apple Watch Workout app intervals vs a timer app](/blog/posts/apple-watch-workout-app-intervals-vs-timer-app).

## Keep the phone close and the speaker off

Apple says the watch is most efficient on Bluetooth to the iPhone it is paired with. Leave the phone in range when the battery is the limit. If it has to stay in a locker, Wi-Fi uses less power than cellular, and a weak signal uses more than a strong one. Use Airplane Mode when you already know there is no coverage. What still times correctly once the phone is gone is the checklist in [using an Apple Watch interval timer without your phone](/blog/posts/apple-watch-interval-timer-without-phone).

Away from the iPhone, Low Power Mode also turns off Wi-Fi and cellular until an app asks for a connection. Apple says turning the radio back on for that can cause significant battery use, and missed calls and notifications come back once an hour. A timer that is already on the watch does not need that radio. A stream or a message check does.

Send the cues to headphones, not the watch speaker. That is the same guidance Apple gives for music.

## Let the cue replace the glance

Raising your wrist to read the round wakes the display. The way off that loop is a cue you do not have to look at.

Start the saved workout on the Apple Watch, so the tap lands on the wrist that is training. Turn on Prominent Haptic in Sounds & Haptics, and snug the band. Peak Interval taps at each phase change. A preparation phase at the front, about 10 seconds, gets you into the first position before work starts, so the opening seconds are not spent staring at the watch.

In a quiet room, use the vibration-only setup in the [silent interval timer](/blog/posts/silent-interval-timer-vibration-only) guide. If you want the phase name as well as the tap, headphones paired to the Watch carry the words without the speaker. The structure of a run is a different job, in [how to build a running interval workout on Apple Watch](/blog/posts/how-to-build-a-running-interval-workout-on-apple-watch).

Run one round with your eyes off the screen. You should feel or hear the change. If you had to look, that phase has no cue, and you will keep waking the display to find out where you are.

## Before a long session

Do this while you are still at the bench.

Apple shows a Low Power Mode alert when the battery reaches 10 percent. A long session that starts on that alert is already late. Charge first.

Turn Low Power Mode on and look for the yellow ring. Confirm the iPhone is in Bluetooth range, or that you are on a Wi-Fi network the phone has joined before. Send audio to headphones, or drop to the haptic.

With watchOS 27, Apple says the watch can pause features you rarely use and show an Extending Battery notice that names the feature. Read that notice before you start. Its Settings button opens the feature, so you can turn a cue back on if the watch paused it. Apple does not publish a fixed list of what gets paused, so the notice is the source.

If the battery only sagged right after a watchOS update, Apple says update work can keep running in the background for a few days. Before you blame the timer, open Settings, Battery, Battery Health, and read Maximum Capacity. That figure is the cell.

You are done when the last round still cues you and you did not wake the screen to learn which phase you were in. Same workout. Less of the watch left on around it.
